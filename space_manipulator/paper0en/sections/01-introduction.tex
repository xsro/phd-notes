\section{Introduction}
Free-floating space manipulators are enabling technologies for on-orbit servicing, debris removal, and spacecraft maintenance. Before contact with a target, however, the manipulator base is not rigidly supported. Joint motion therefore produces a reaction motion of the base through conservation of momentum, and the end-effector motion is coupled to the motion of the entire spacecraft--manipulator system. This coupling, together with nonlinear dynamics, uncertain parameters, and external disturbances, makes accurate end-effector tracking substantially more difficult than tracking with a fixed-base robot. The difficulty is increased when the tracking error must be reduced within a time specified by the designer rather than by the controller gains or the initial condition.

The coupled kinematics of space manipulators can be described by a generalized Jacobian~\cite{umetani1989}, while the virtual-manipulator formulation provides an equivalent representation of their kinematic and dynamic coupling~\cite{vafa1990}. Fundamental properties of free-floating systems, including dynamic singularities, were investigated in~\cite{papadopoulos1993,dubowsky1993}. These results have supported subsequent developments in adaptive control and reactionless-motion control~\cite{parlaktuna2004,xu2016}. Under the momentum conditions considered in this paper, the base motion can be eliminated, yielding an equivalent joint-space model of the form
\begin{equation}
 H_g(q)\ddot q+C_g(q,\dot q)\dot q=\tau_m.
\label{eq:intro_reduced_dynamics}
\end{equation}
This reduction is useful for controller design, but it does not remove the effects of model uncertainty and lumped disturbances.

A range of methods has been developed for trajectory tracking of free-floating robots, including optimization-based planning, model predictive control, time-delay estimation, adaptive sliding-mode control, and prescribed-performance control~\cite{wang2015,ning2019,yu2022,yao2021}. These methods can provide robustness or transient constraints; nevertheless, their convergence guarantees are generally expressed in terms of tunable gains, initial conditions, or performance functions. Fixed-time and prescribed-time control addresses this limitation by allowing a convergence bound to be selected independently of the initial condition~\cite{polyakov2012,zuo2019}. Applications to robot manipulators and space robots have been reported in~\cite{su2020,xuPredefinedTimeTrackingControl2024,li2025}. Many such designs, however, rely on nonsmooth terminal terms or fractional powers, which may lead to discontinuous control inputs, gain singularities, or implementation difficulties near the terminal time.

Delayed feedback offers an alternative design mechanism. Since the introduction of delayed self-feedback~\cite{pyragas1992}, delay-based methods have been used to stabilize systems without requiring discontinuous terminal nonlinearities. In particular, smooth periodic delayed feedback (PDF) can achieve fixed-time stabilization of controllable linear time-delay systems through the annihilation of a finite-dimensional one-period state-transition operator~\cite{zhouFixedtimeStabilizationLinear2022}. Extending this mechanism to a nonlinear free-floating manipulator is nontrivial: the nonlinear terms and disturbances must first be mapped into a sufficiently accurate auxiliary input channel, and the effect of imperfect compensation must be quantified. A prescribed-time disturbance observer is a natural interface for this purpose~\cite{jiangPrescribedtimeDisturbanceObserver2024}.

The above observations motivate the following question: can a free-floating space manipulator be transformed into a controllable error channel for smooth PDF control while retaining an explicit prescribed-time guarantee in the ideal case and a quantitative bound in the practical case? This paper answers this question for the reduced joint-space model in~\eqref{eq:intro_reduced_dynamics}.

The main contributions are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item A momentum-based reduction is formulated to explicitly relate the base reaction motion to an equivalent joint-space model, thereby providing a clear control-oriented representation of the free-floating manipulator.
    \item A prescribed-time disturbance observer and a two-stage timing scheme are developed. The observer time and the subsequent tracking time are specified independently, which makes the overall timing requirement explicit.
    \item A dynamic compensation and normalization procedure converts the nonlinear tracking problem into a controllable auxiliary error channel suitable for smooth periodic delayed feedback.
    \item A Gramian-based gain construction is provided, together with stability analysis and explicit practical error bounds that account for observation error, model mismatch, actuator limitations, and numerical discretization.
    \item MATLAB simulations on a nominal seven-degree-of-freedom equivalent model illustrate the feasibility of the proposed design under bounded disturbances.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:modeling} derives the free-floating manipulator model. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formulates the tracking problem and states the assumptions. Section~\ref{sec:controller} presents the disturbance observer, dynamic compensation, and PDF controller. Section~\ref{sec:stability} establishes the convergence and practical error bounds. Section~\ref{sec:simulation} reports the simulation results, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.
