\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This note developed a prescribed-performance fencing framework for
double-integrator multi-agent systems.  By applying PPC to the enclosure
error, the transient and steady-state behavior of the target-to-centroid
error can be specified through a user-chosen envelope $\rho(t)$.  The
centroid-level design combines this soft performance requirement with
zero-sum local terms for artificial-potential collision avoidance and
velocity matching.

An APF-filtered individual PPC algorithm was also introduced.  This design
regulates locally filtered target-relative errors and uses the zero-sum
property of the APF filter to recover a centroid-level PPC bound.  Its main
advantage is that the controller contains the APF term but does not require
the derivative of the APF, which simplifies implementation when the
repulsive potential depends on switching active neighbor sets.

For the case where the target acceleration and disturbances are not
available, an adaptive robust variant was developed.  This controller treats
$u_0+d_0$ and $d_i$ as a bounded lumped uncertainty and estimates the
required sliding gain online.  Thus the PPC fencing objective can still be
maintained without feedforward cancellation of the target acceleration.

Future work will focus on a composite Lyapunov analysis of the coupled
estimation, PPC, and APF-filter dynamics; actuator-saturation-aware
prescribed-time designs; extensions to unknown or non-matching target
accelerations; and distributed implementations over time-varying
communication graphs.
