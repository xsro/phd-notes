\author{Liu Cheng}

\begin{abstract}
This note studies prescribed-performance target fencing for multi-agent
systems with double-integrator dynamics.  The objective is to drive a swarm
so that a moving target is enclosed by the agents' convex hull, while all
pairwise distances remain above a prescribed safety margin and agent
velocities match the target velocity.  Existing fencing protocols typically
provide asymptotic enclosure, but their transient overshoot, convergence
rate, and steady-state accuracy are not specified a priori.  To address this
limitation, we combine prescribed-performance control with an
artificial-potential collision-avoidance fence.  Two controller structures
are presented: a centroid-level PPC design, an APF-filtered individual PPC
design that avoids differentiating the repulsive potential term, and an
adaptive variant for unknown target acceleration and unknown disturbances.
The resulting framework gives an explicit envelope for the enclosure error
while preserving collision avoidance under standard barrier assumptions.
\end{abstract}
